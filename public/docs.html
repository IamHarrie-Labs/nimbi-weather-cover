What Nimbi actually does

A plain explanation, no jargon. If anything here is still unclear, that's a gap in this page, not in your understanding.

The basic idea

Nimbi sells cover against a temperature. You pick a place, a temperature line, and a date. If the real temperature crosses that line by that date, you get paid. If it doesn't, you don't. That part is standard.

The part that isn't standard is how Nimbi decides what the "real temperature" is. It doesn't trust one weather source. It asks six of them, at the same time, the same question, and only acts when a real majority of them agree with each other.

Why one source isn't enough

Any single weather feed can go stale, glitch, or just be wrong, and nothing downstream would notice. We found this happening for real while building Nimbi: one of our six oracles, called chainsight-oracle, reported the exact same temperature (40.50°C) for three completely different cities, on the same day.

Cairo, London, and Reykjavik do not share a climate. A source that says the same number for all three isn't measuring anything. It's stuck.

Nimbi catches this automatically. It doesn't need to know in advance which oracle is broken. It just looks at where the readings cluster, finds the group that agrees with each other, and treats anything far outside that group as unreliable for this particular question, right now.

How a settlement actually happens

Every time it's time to check a policy, three things happen in order:

1. Read. The agent asks all six oracles the same question about the same place, at the same time.
2. Consensus. It checks whether a real majority of them landed close together. If chainsight-oracle (or any other source) is off on its own, it gets named and excluded, not averaged in.
3. Settle. If a majority agreed and the line was crossed, the payout happens immediately, on-chain. If not, nothing moves, and the reason gets written down permanently.

The three outcomes, plainly

Paid out means a majority of oracles agreed and the temperature crossed your line. Money moves right away.

Checked means a majority agreed, but the temperature never crossed your line. Nothing is owed. This isn't a failure, it's just a policy that didn't trigger.

Held means the oracles couldn't agree, or too few of them answered. Nimbi refuses to guess. Your money stays exactly where it was, and the specific reason ("no majority", "spread too wide", and so on) is recorded on-chain for anyone to check.

Why "Held" is the important one

Most systems either pay out on bad data or quietly retry until something looks plausible. Nimbi does neither. A "Held" result is Nimbi correctly noticing it doesn't have good enough information to act, and saying so in public, on the blockchain, rather than guessing with your money.

This is a testnet demonstration, not insurance. It runs on Base Sepolia using play-money USDC that anyone can mint for free. There is no real risk transfer, no underwriting, and no regulatory product behind it. It exists to show that consensus across independent sources is a workable, checkable way to decide when to move money automatically.

The contract address and every settlement it has ever made are public. You can verify all of this yourself rather than take our word for it.
